How to make phone battery last longer: 9 settings that actually work

Your battery isn't dying because your phone is old. It's dying because of a handful of settings that quietly drain it from morning to night.

Focus keyword: make phone battery last longer

Tue 10 Nov 20266 min readBy Noman

If your phone is at 20% by mid-afternoon, the usual advice — “turn everything off and dim the screen to nothing” — makes the phone miserable to use. You don't need to do that. Battery drain is almost always caused by a few specific things, and fixing just those gets you most of the benefit with none of the pain. The nine fixes below are how to make phone battery last longer in real life — most people who work through them gain two to four extra hours a day.

First, find out who's actually guilty

Before changing anything, look at the evidence. On Android: Settings → Battery → Battery usage. On iPhone: Settings → Battery, then scroll down. You'll see exactly which apps used your battery in the last 24 hours. Most people find one or two surprises at the top — a social app, a game left running, or a shopping app checking location all day. Everything below targets those culprits.

The 9 settings, in order of impact

  1. Stop background refresh for greedy apps. iPhone: Settings → General → Background App Refresh — switch off apps that don't need live updates. Android: Settings → Apps → (the app) → Battery → Restricted. Games and shopping apps almost never need to run in the background.
  2. Turn on Adaptive Battery / Optimised Charging. Both Android and iPhone can learn your routine and slow-charge overnight, which dramatically slows battery ageing.
  3. Cut screen timeout to 30 seconds. The screen is the biggest single drain. Settings → Display → Screen timeout. You won't notice 30 seconds; your battery will.
  4. Use dark mode if you have an OLED screen. On OLED displays (most phones from the last few years), black pixels are literally switched off. Dark mode can cut screen power use noticeably.
  5. Turn off location for apps that don't need it. Set location to “While using the app” for everything except maps. Weather, shopping and social apps do not need your location 24 hours a day.
  6. Disable push for email you don't need instantly. Set less important accounts to fetch every hour instead. Mail checking every few minutes, all day, is a silent drain.
  7. Remove home-screen widgets you never look at. Weather, news and stock widgets refresh constantly. Keep the ones you use; delete the decoration.
  8. Turn off 5G where coverage is weak. A phone hunting for a weak 5G signal burns far more power than steady 4G/LTE. Indoors or in rural areas, LTE-only mode is often the smarter setting.
  9. Check battery health once. iPhone: Settings → Battery → Battery Health. Android: Settings → Battery → Battery diagnostics (wording varies). Below 80% health, no setting will save you — a battery replacement is cheaper than a new phone.

Two everyday habits that quietly undo all of this

Settings do most of the work, but two habits can quietly cancel them out. The first is heat. A phone left on a sunny car dashboard, gaming while fast-charging, or charging tucked under a pillow ages its battery faster than almost anything else — if the back feels hot to the touch, take the case off while it charges and give it a short break. The second is hotspot duty: tethering a laptop for hours runs the phone’s radios at full power and can drain even a healthy battery by mid-afternoon. If you tether every day, plug the phone in while you do it, and switch the hotspot off the moment you finish — it is surprising how many mystery drains turn out to be a hotspot left running in a pocket.

Make phone battery last longer with one charging habit

Lithium batteries age fastest when kept at 100% or run to 0%. You don't need to obsess, but the easy version is: charge a little before it gets desperate, unplug once it's comfortably full, and let optimised overnight charging do the slow top-up. Do the nine fixes above plus that one habit, and “battery anxiety” mostly disappears.

What success actually looks like

Do not expect miracles — expect a calmer day. A typical result looks like this: a phone that used to hit 20% by mid-afternoon now reaches the evening with 35–45% still in hand, and charging becomes a relaxed top-up rather than an emergency hunt for a socket and a borrowed cable. Give the changes three normal days before judging them, because your daily usage varies more than any single setting does. And if nothing improves, go back to step nine and check the battery health figure again: once it falls below about 80%, the battery itself is the problem, and a replacement through the manufacturer or a reputable repair shop restores the phone for a fraction of the price of a new one.

The short version

  • The screen and background apps — not the battery itself — cause most drain.
  • Turning off background refresh for your 5 greediest apps is the single biggest win.
  • Charge between roughly 20% and 85% to keep the battery healthy for years.
