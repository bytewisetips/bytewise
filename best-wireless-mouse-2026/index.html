Best wireless mouse 2026: 5 picks for work, gaming and travel

A good wireless mouse is the cheapest upgrade your desk will ever get. These are the five best wireless mice of 2026 — for work, gaming, and travel.

Sat 28 Nov 20266 min readBy Noman

The mouse that came free with your computer is probably the worst thing on your desk. A good wireless mouse costs less than a takeaway, lasts months on a battery, and makes every hour at the computer more comfortable — no cable dragging, no wrist ache, no lag when you need precision. The best wireless mouse 2026 has to offer depends on what you do: quiet office mouse, precise gaming mouse, or tiny travel mouse. These five picks cover all of it, from £25 to £80.

What to look for in a wireless mouse

Four things decide whether a wireless mouse is a joy or a nuisance. First, the shape: it should fill your palm without forcing your wrist into a claw — try one in a shop first if you can, or buy from somewhere with easy returns. Second, the connection: Bluetooth is simplest and works with laptops and tablets, while a tiny USB receiver is more reliable for gaming — the best mice offer both. Third, battery life: a good mouse lasts three to six months on one AA battery, or weeks on a charge if it is rechargeable, and a mouse that needs charging every few days will drive you mad. And fourth, the sensor: for office work almost any modern sensor is fine, but for gaming you want at least 4000 DPI and one that does not stutter when you move fast.

The 5 best wireless mice of 2026

1. Logitech MX Master 3S — around £80

The pick for most people who work at a computer all day. The MX Master 3S is shaped to fit a right hand properly, with a thumb rest and a second scroll wheel for sideways scrolling. It is silent — the clicks make almost no sound — and it connects to three devices at once, so one mouse runs your laptop, your desktop, and your tablet. The battery lasts up to 70 days, and it charges over USB-C. It is big, so it is not a travel mouse — but for a desk, it is the best wireless mouse you can buy.

2. Logitech G502 X Lightspeed — around £70

The gaming pick. The G502 X is a proper gaming mouse: a sensor that tracks flawlessly at speed, switches you can customise, and a battery that lasts around 140 hours of play. It is comfortable for long sessions, and the extra buttons are useful once you set them up. It is overkill if you only play occasionally — but if you game most evenings, this is the one to buy.

3. Logitech Pebble M350 — around £25

The travel pick. The Pebble is small, flat, and light enough to disappear into a laptop bag, and it comes in colours that do not look like office equipment. It runs for up to 18 months on a single AA battery, connects over Bluetooth or USB, and is quiet enough for a library. The trade-off is the size: if you have large hands, it will feel cramped after an hour. But for working from cafes and hotel rooms, nothing beats it for the price.

4. Razer Pro Click Mini — around £50

The pick if you want one mouse for work and play. The Razer Pro Click Mini is small enough to travel with but comfortable for a full working day, and its silent switches keep it office-friendly. It connects to four devices at once — Bluetooth and a USB receiver — and the battery lasts for months. It is the best all-rounder here — not the best at any one thing, but the one that does everything well.

5. Microsoft Bluetooth Ergonomic Mouse — around £40

The budget ergonomic pick. The Microsoft Ergonomic Mouse is shaped to keep your wrist in a more natural position, which matters if you get wrist ache from a flat mouse. It is simple: no extra buttons, no software — it just connects over Bluetooth and works. The battery lasts around 15 months, and at around £40 it is the cheapest way to find out whether an ergonomic shape helps you. The only catch: it is right-handed only, and it is not a gaming mouse.

Bluetooth or USB receiver — which is better?

For most people it does not matter much — both work fine for office work and browsing. Bluetooth is the tidier option: no dongle to lose, and it works with tablets and phones too. A USB receiver is slightly more responsive, which is why gamers prefer it, and it works with computers that have flaky Bluetooth. The practical answer is to buy a mouse that offers both, like most of the picks above, so you can use Bluetooth on the move and the receiver at your desk. If you do use a receiver, keep it plugged in — the tiny ones are easy to lose, and replacements cost more than you would expect.

Best wireless mouse 2026: the short version

For most people, the Logitech MX Master 3S at around £80 is the best wireless mouse of 2026 — comfortable, silent, and it connects to three devices. Gamers should look at the G502 X at around £70, and travellers at the Pebble at around £25. Whatever you buy, pick a shape that fits your hand, make sure it offers both Bluetooth and a USB receiver, and check the battery life first.

Frequently asked questions

Is a wireless mouse as good as a wired one?

For office work and everyday use, yes — modern wireless mice are just as fast and reliable. Gamers who play competitively still prefer a USB receiver or a wired mouse for the lowest possible delay.

How long does a wireless mouse battery last?

A good office mouse lasts three to six months on one AA battery, or up to 70 days on a charge if it is rechargeable. Gaming mice need charging more often — usually every week or two.

Can one wireless mouse work with two computers?

Yes — many mice, including the Logitech MX Master 3S and the Razer Pro Click Mini, connect to two or three devices at once, and you switch between them with a button on the mouse.

Do I need a mouse pad for a wireless mouse?

Not usually — modern sensors work fine on a desk. A mouse pad helps on glass desks, where sensors struggle, and it protects the desk surface over time.
