Are gaming phones worth it in 2026? REDMAGIC 12 Pro goes on sale this week

Gaming phones promise console power in your pocket. For most people that is not the point — here is who they actually suit.

Mon 16 Nov 20264 min readBy Noman

Are gaming phones worth it? It is a fair question, and this week is a good time to ask it: the REDMAGIC 12 Pro, the latest gaming phone, goes on sale worldwide on 18 November from $949, with a 185Hz screen, liquid cooling and an 8,400mAh battery — numbers no normal flagship phone can match. Gaming phones are built for one job: playing demanding games at full speed for hours without slowing down. But most people do not play demanding games for hours, and the things that make a gaming phone great at games can make it worse at everything else. Here is how to tell which side you are on.

What a gaming phone actually gives you

Start with the screen. A gaming phone's display refreshes 144 or 185 times a second, where a normal phone manages 60 or 120 — and in a fast game, that makes movement look noticeably smoother. Then there is cooling: gaming phones have fans or liquid cooling built in, so the chip stays fast during a long session instead of slowing down as it heats up. The battery is usually bigger too — 8,000mAh or more, against 5,000mAh in a normal flagship — because games drain power fast. And most gaming phones add shoulder triggers: extra buttons on the edge that work like a controller's triggers, which serious mobile gamers swear by. None of this is marketing fluff; it all makes a real difference while you play.

The REDMAGIC 12 Pro in short

This week's example shows the pattern clearly. The REDMAGIC 12 Pro goes on sale worldwide on 18 November, from $949 in the US and €899 in Europe, with a 185Hz display, an 8,400mAh battery and a liquid-cooled chip built to run flat out. It is, on paper, one of the fastest phones you can buy at any price. It is also a phone with no wireless charging, a camera that reviewers usually describe as decent rather than great, and software that is focused on games first and everything else second. That trade-off — maximum gaming power, ordinary everything else — is the whole story of gaming phones.

Who a gaming phone is actually for

A gaming phone makes sense if you play competitive mobile games most days — the fast shooters and racing games where a smoother screen and a cooler chip genuinely change the result. It also suits people who play for long stretches away from a charger, because the huge battery means a three-hour session barely dents it. And it suits anyone who wants flagship speed without the flagship price: a gaming phone often costs hundreds less than a top Samsung or iPhone while matching them on raw performance. If that sounds like you, a gaming phone is not a gimmick — it is the right tool for the job.

Who should skip one

If you mostly play casual games — puzzles, word games, the odd round of something light — a gaming phone buys you nothing. Your games do not need a 185Hz screen or liquid cooling, and any mid-range phone runs them perfectly. If the camera matters to you, skip one too: gaming phones spend their budget on the chip and the screen, so the camera is usually a step behind a flagship at the same price. And if you want a phone that feels premium in the hand, gaming phones are often thicker, heavier and louder-looking than a normal phone, with RGB lights and vents that not everyone wants on show.

The downsides nobody mentions

The first downside is software updates. Gaming phone brands are smaller than Samsung or Apple, and their phones often get fewer years of updates — which matters if you plan to keep the phone for three or four years. The second is resale value: gaming phones lose value faster than a flagship iPhone or Samsung, so the money you save at the start partly disappears at the end. And the third is the everyday experience. A gaming phone's software is full of game modes, performance sliders and pop-ups that you have to turn off or live with — fine if you love tweaking, tiring if you just want a phone that stays out of the way.

Are gaming phones worth it? The short version

So, are gaming phones worth it? If you play demanding games most days, yes — the REDMAGIC 12 Pro and its rivals give you flagship speed, a huge battery and a smoother screen for less money than a flagship, and the trade-offs are ones you will barely notice. If you play casually, or the camera and the long software support matter more, buy a normal flagship or a good mid-range phone instead. The right answer depends on how you actually use your phone — not on how impressive the spec sheet looks.

The short version

  • Gaming phones win on screen speed, cooling and battery — the three things that matter while playing.
  • They cost less than flagships for the same raw performance, but the camera and software support are weaker.
  • Buy one if you play demanding games most days; otherwise a normal phone is the better buy.

Frequently asked questions

Do gaming phones work as normal everyday phones?

Yes. They make calls, run apps and browse exactly like any other Android phone — the gaming features are extras on top, and you can ignore them when you are not playing.

Is a 185Hz screen worth it if I don't play games?

No. Outside games, the difference between 120Hz and 185Hz is barely visible, and the faster screen drains the battery quicker for no benefit.

How long does a gaming phone battery last?

Longer than a normal flagship — the 8,000mAh-plus batteries in most gaming phones easily last a full day of heavy use, and often two days of light use.

Are gaming phones good value compared to a flagship?

For raw speed, yes — they often match flagship performance for hundreds less. But flagships give you a better camera, longer software support and better resale value for the extra money.
