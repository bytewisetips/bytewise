AirPods vs Galaxy Buds: which earbuds should you buy in 2026?

The two best-known earbuds fight the same battle: which one fits your phone? The answer is simpler than the adverts make it sound.

Focus keyword: AirPods vs Galaxy Buds

Sun 15 Nov 20265 min readBy Noman

AirPods vs Galaxy Buds is the earbud question almost everyone asks — and the honest answer is that the best pair for you probably depends on the phone already in your pocket. Both are excellent: good sound, solid battery life, and clever features that make everyday listening easier. But each one is designed to work best inside its own world, and the features that make one pair shine can quietly disappear on the other side. Here is how they actually compare.

The short answer: match your earbuds to your phone

If you have an iPhone, AirPods are the easy choice. They pair instantly, switch automatically between your iPhone, iPad and Mac, and every feature works out of the box. If you have a Samsung phone, Galaxy Buds do the same trick: instant pairing, automatic switching between your phone and tablet, and settings that live right in your phone's menu. Buy the pair that matches your phone and you get the full experience with no fiddling. Buy the other pair and the earbuds still work — but you lose the clever bits that make them worth the money.

Sound quality: closer than you would think

Put both pairs on and the difference is smaller than the internet argues about. AirPods tend to sound clean and balanced, with clear voices and a smooth top end — great for podcasts and calls. Galaxy Buds usually push the bass a little harder, which makes pop and hip-hop feel fuller, though it can muddy quieter music. Both support their own high-quality audio modes, but here is the catch: those modes only work fully with their own brand of phone. On the other side, you get good standard sound, not the best either pair can do. For most people, either pair sounds good enough that the phone match matters more than the sound.

Noise cancelling: both do the job

The Pro-level models of both — AirPods Pro and Galaxy Buds Pro — cancel noise properly: they hush a train, a plane cabin or an office hum to a soft background murmur. The cheaper models of both brands cancel a little noise or none at all, relying on the fit in your ear instead. In practice, the two Pro models are close enough that you would struggle to pick a winner in a blind test. What matters more is the fit: try both shapes if you can, because the pair that seals better in your ear will cancel more noise, whatever the box promises.

Battery life and charging

Both pairs last around five to six hours on a charge with noise cancelling on, and the case tops them up three or four more times, taking the total to roughly 24 to 30 hours. Galaxy Buds often edge slightly ahead on the headline number, but the difference is minutes, not hours — you will charge the case at the weekend either way. Both cases charge over USB-C, and both support wireless charging on the Pro models. One nice Samsung trick: you can charge Galaxy Buds from the back of a Samsung phone, which has saved more than one commute when the case was nearly empty.

The features that only work on one side

This is where the phone match really shows. AirPods on an iPhone give you automatic switching, Find My tracking, hands-free Siri and spatial audio that follows your head. Galaxy Buds on a Samsung phone give you 360 audio, switching between your Samsung devices, Find My Earbuds and deep controls inside the settings app. Use AirPods with an Android phone and you lose Siri, automatic switching and most of the settings — you get basic Bluetooth earbuds. Use Galaxy Buds with an iPhone and you lose the good audio mode, the earbud finder and most of the extras. The earbuds still play music fine, but you are paying flagship prices for basic features.

Price: which is better value?

Galaxy Buds are usually the cheaper pair. The standard Galaxy Buds often sell for £50 to £80 less than the equivalent AirPods, and Samsung runs frequent sales that push the price down further. AirPods hold their price more firmly, and even the basic AirPods cost more than the equivalent Galaxy Buds. If you are buying for the phone you own, the price gap is not the deciding factor — the features are. But if you are buying a gift, or your phone loyalty is loose, the Galaxy Buds are the better-value buy for the same everyday experience.

Which pair should you buy?

If you own an iPhone, buy AirPods. If you own a Samsung or another Android phone, buy Galaxy Buds. It really is that simple. The AirPods vs Galaxy Buds debate only gets complicated when you try to mix the two — and then the answer is whichever one matches the phone you will actually use them with. Both are excellent earbuds, both sound great, and both will last you years. The wrong choice is not a bad pair of earbuds; it is the right pair for the wrong phone.

The short version

  • Match the earbuds to your phone: AirPods for iPhone, Galaxy Buds for Samsung and Android.
  • Sound and noise cancelling are close — the features you lose on the wrong phone are the real difference.
  • Galaxy Buds are usually £50 to £80 cheaper, so they are the better-value buy if your phone loyalty is loose.
