REDMAGIC 12 Pro goes on sale today: is it worth it for gamers?

The most extreme gaming phone of the year is on sale from today. Here is the honest version of what it does — and who should skip it.

Wed 18 Nov 20264 min readBy Noman

The REDMAGIC 12 Pro goes on open sale today — Wednesday 18 November — and if you play games on your phone, this is the most extreme option you can buy right now. REDMAGIC announced it globally on 3 November, early-bird buyers got in yesterday, and from today anyone can order one. The headline numbers are hard to ignore: a screen that refreshes 185 times a second, a battery bigger than most tablets carry, and a cooling system with an actual fan inside the phone. Prices start at $949 in the US, £799 in the UK and €899 in Europe. So is it worth it — or is it a specialist toy for a tiny audience? Here is the honest version.

What you get for $949

Start with the parts that matter for gaming. The REDMAGIC 12 Pro runs on Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 chip, with up to 16GB of RAM. The base model pairs 12GB of RAM with 256GB of storage for $949 — the 16GB/512GB model costs $1,049 and the top 16GB/1TB model costs $1,199. The screen is a 6.85-inch AMOLED panel that refreshes up to 185 times a second, and the battery is a huge 8,400mAh, charged at up to 80W whether you plug in or use a wireless charger. There is a 50MP main camera and a 50MP ultra-wide camera, a 16MP selfie camera hidden under the display, and REDMAGIC OS 12, based on Android 17. It is even rated IP68/IP69 against water and dust, which is rare for a gaming phone.

The screen and the battery are the real story

Most flagship phones refresh their screens 120 times a second, and the jump to 185Hz is genuinely noticeable in fast games — movement looks smoother and your reactions feel a fraction quicker. The battery is the bigger deal for most people: 8,400mAh is the largest battery in any mainstream phone on sale today, and it should last two days of normal use or a full day of heavy gaming. When it does run low, 80W charging — wired or wireless — fills it fast. One catch: the standard box does not include a wall charger, so you will need your own 80W charger or buy one separately.

The cooling fan: clever, loud, and a little strange

The REDMAGIC 12 Pro is the only phone you can buy with a fan inside it. Its AquaCore 3.0 cooling system pairs two liquid-cooling loops and a vapour chamber with a fan that spins at up to 25,500 RPM, and the transparent back panel lets you watch it work. That sounds like a gimmick, but it solves a real problem: most phones slow down after twenty minutes of demanding gaming because they get too hot, and this one is built to hold full speed for much longer. The trade-offs are real, though. The fan is audible in a quiet room, it is a moving part that can wear out or collect dust, and the glowing design is not subtle. If you love the look, it is a feature; if you want a phone that disappears into your pocket, it is a reason to look elsewhere.

Who should actually buy a REDMAGIC 12 Pro?

This phone is for one group of people: serious mobile gamers. If you play Genshin Impact, PUBG Mobile or Call of Duty: Mobile for hours at a time and you want the smoothest frame rates and the longest battery, nothing else on sale today matches it for the price. It is not for everyone, though. The cameras are fine — good daylight photos, decent video — but they are not in the same league as a Samsung or an iPhone, so if photography is your priority, your money goes further elsewhere. The software is gamer-focused and busy, and REDMAGIC promises two Android updates plus five years of security updates — decent, but well behind the seven years Samsung and Google now offer. Buy it for the gaming and the battery, not for the camera or the software support.

What to check before you order

Four checks before you spend anything. First, the price in your region: the US store lists $949, $1,049 and $1,199, while the UK price starts at £799 and Europe at €899 — check your local store rather than trusting a price converted from another currency. Second, the charger: the box has no wall charger, so budget for an 80W one if you do not already own one. Third, the network bands, especially if you are importing — a phone bought for another region may miss the bands your carrier uses for the fastest data. And fourth, the returns policy: the fan, the size and the software are all things you will only know you like once the phone is in your hand.

REDMAGIC 12 Pro: the short version

So, is the REDMAGIC 12 Pro worth it? If you are a serious mobile gamer, yes — from $949 you get the fastest screen, the biggest battery and the most serious cooling of any phone on sale today, and nothing else matches that combination at this price. If you mostly scroll, message and take photos, no — a normal flagship will serve you better, with a better camera, cleaner software and longer updates. The open sale starts today, so there is no rush: read a couple of hands-on reviews, check the price in your country, and buy it because you actually need what it does.

The short version

  • The REDMAGIC 12 Pro goes on open sale today, from $949 in the US, £799 in the UK and €899 in Europe.
  • Its real strengths are the 185Hz screen, the huge 8,400mAh battery and the fan cooling that keeps games running at full speed.
  • Buy it if you are a serious mobile gamer; skip it if the camera, clean software or long updates matter more to you.

How much does the REDMAGIC 12 Pro cost?

From $949 in the US (12GB + 256GB), £799 in the UK and €899 in Europe. The 16GB + 512GB model costs $1,049 and the top 16GB + 1TB model costs $1,199.

When does the REDMAGIC 12 Pro go on sale?

Open sales start today, Wednesday 18 November 2026, from 7am Eastern time. Anyone can order one now — no voucher or early access is needed.

Does the REDMAGIC 12 Pro come with a charger in the box?

No. The standard box has no wall charger, so you need your own 80W USB-C charger or you have to buy one separately.

Is the REDMAGIC 12 Pro good for everyday use, not just gaming?

Yes, for battery life and speed, but the cameras are only average and the software is gamer-focused. If gaming is not your priority, a normal flagship is a better buy.
