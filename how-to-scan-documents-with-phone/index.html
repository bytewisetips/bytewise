How to scan documents with your phone (iPhone and Android)

Your phone is already a decent scanner — it just hides the feature. Here is where to find it on iPhone and Android, and how to get a clean result.

Sun 22 Nov 20265 min readBy Noman

A form arrives by email, you need to sign it and send it back, and there is no scanner in sight. Your phone can do the job — and not with a shaky photo, but with a proper, straightened scan saved as a PDF. Both iPhone and Android have a scanner built in, free, and most people never notice it. Here is how to scan documents with your phone, on either system, and how to get a result that actually looks like a scan.

Scan on iPhone with the Notes app

On an iPhone, the scanner lives inside the Notes app, which is already on your phone. Open Notes, start a new note, then tap the paperclip or the camera icon and choose "Scan Documents". Point the camera at the document and hold the phone steady — the app finds the edges by itself, highlights them in yellow, and takes the picture automatically. If it grabs the wrong edges, drag the corners to fix them before you save. You can scan several pages into one document: just keep pointing at the next page. When you are done, tap Save, and the whole thing is stored in the note as a PDF you can share, print, or save to Files. It is the quickest route on an iPhone, and it costs nothing.

Scan on Android with Google Drive

On Android, the easiest built-in scanner is in Google Drive, which is on almost every Android phone. Open Drive, tap the camera icon at the bottom right (or the plus button, then Scan), and point the camera at your document. Drive finds the edges, crops the page, and lets you adjust the corners before saving. You can add more pages the same way, and when you finish, the scan is saved as a PDF straight into your Drive, ready to share or download. Some phones offer a scanner in their own apps too — Samsung's Camera app has a "Scan document" option that appears when it spots a page — but Google Drive works on any Android phone, so it is the one to remember.

Use your camera app for a quick scan

If you only need one page and you are in a hurry, your normal camera app will do — with a small trick. Take the photo from directly above the document, in good light, and make sure the whole page is inside the frame. Then open the photo, crop it to the page edges, and use the edit tools to bump up the brightness and contrast a little — that turns a grey, shadowy photo into something that reads like a scan. On iPhone, the Photos app can straighten a tilted page for you; on Android, Google Photos has the same crop and straighten tools. This is not as tidy as the built-in scanner, but for a receipt or a single form it is fast and good enough.

Tips for a clean scan

A good scan is mostly about light and position, and four small things make a big difference. First, put the document on a flat surface with a plain, dark background — a dark table or a plain folder makes the edges easy for the camera to find. Second, use daylight or a bright room light, and stand so your shadow does not fall across the page. Third, hold the phone directly above the document, not at an angle: the straighter you hold it, the less the app has to correct. And fourth, check the result before you send it — zoom in and make sure the text is sharp and nothing is cut off at the edges. If a page comes out blurry, wipe the camera lens (a smudged lens is the most common cause) and try again.

Save, share and organise your scans

Once you have a scan, treat it like the real document. Give the PDF a clear name — "passport-application-form" beats "Scan 2026-11-22" — so you can find it later. On iPhone, share it from Notes to Files or straight into an email; on Android, it is already in Google Drive, where you can rename it and move it into a folder. For anything important, keep a copy somewhere safe: a signed contract or an ID document deserves a backup, not just a place on your phone. And if you scan the same kind of document often — receipts, bills, school forms — make a folder for them now, so next time the scan goes straight where it belongs.

How to scan documents with your phone: the short version

On iPhone, open Notes, start a new note, tap the camera icon and choose "Scan Documents" — the app finds the page edges and saves a PDF. On Android, open Google Drive, tap the camera icon, and scan the same way. For one quick page, your camera app plus a crop and a brightness boost will do. Scan in good light, hold the phone straight above the page, check the result, and save the PDF with a clear name.

The short version

  • iPhone: scan from the Notes app (camera icon, then "Scan Documents"). Android: scan from Google Drive (camera icon, then Scan). Both save a proper PDF, free.
  • Good light, a flat dark background, and the phone held straight above the page make the difference between a scan and a shaky photo.
  • Name the PDF clearly and keep a backup of anything important — a scan is only useful if you can find it again.

Frequently asked questions

Is scanning with my phone as good as a real scanner?

For everyday documents, yes. A phone scan gives you a clean, straight PDF that is fine for forms, receipts and records. A flatbed scanner is still better for photos and very fine print.

Do I need to download a scanner app?

No. iPhone has a scanner built into Notes, and Android has one in Google Drive. Extra scanner apps exist, but most charge a subscription for features your phone already has.

Can I scan several pages into one PDF?

Yes. Both Notes on iPhone and Drive on Android let you keep adding pages to the same scan, and save the whole thing as one PDF.

Why does my scan look dark or blurry?

Usually it is the light or a smudged lens. Scan in bright light, keep your shadow off the page, wipe the lens, and hold the phone steady directly above the document.
