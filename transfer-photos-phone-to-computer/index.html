How to transfer photos from your phone to your computer: 5 easy ways

Your photos are stuck on your phone until you move them. These five ways get them onto your computer — pick the one that fits your phone.

Thu 19 Nov 20265 min readBy Noman

Your phone holds a holiday's worth of photos, and they exist in exactly one place — until the phone runs out of space, or the phone itself is lost. The fix is simple: there are five easy ways to transfer photos from your phone to your computer, for iPhone and Android alike. Some are fast, some are automatic, and one of them you can set up once and never think about again. Pick the method that fits how many photos you have and how often you want to do it.

1. A USB cable: fastest for hundreds of photos

A cable is still the fastest way to move a lot of photos. Plug your phone into your computer with its charging cable, and on an iPhone tap "Trust This Computer" when it asks — on Android, swipe down the notification and choose "File transfer" instead of "Charging only". On Windows your phone then appears in File Explorer like a memory stick: open it, find the DCIM folder, and copy the photos to a folder on your computer. On a Mac, plug in an iPhone and the Photos app offers to import them; for an Android phone, use the free Android File Transfer app. A cable moves hundreds of full-size photos in minutes, and it works with no internet at all — which is why it is still the best option for a big clear-out.

2. Google Photos or iCloud: set it once, forget it

The easiest method is the one you never have to do. If you use Google Photos, turn on backup in the app and your photos upload automatically whenever you are on Wi-Fi — then open photos.google.com on your computer and download any photo you want. iPhone users get the same thing with iCloud Photos: switch it on in Settings, and your photos appear in the Photos app on your Mac, or at icloud.com on a Windows computer. The catch is storage: Google gives you 15GB free across your whole Google account, and Apple gives you 5GB free, so if you take a lot of photos you will eventually need to pay for more space or delete old backups. But for day-to-day use, automatic backup is the method that actually gets done.

3. AirDrop or Quick Share: wireless, no cable needed

If your phone and computer are the same brand, wireless transfer takes seconds. On an iPhone and a Mac, use AirDrop: select the photos, tap the share button, tap AirDrop, and choose your Mac — the full-size photos arrive in your Downloads folder. On Android and Windows, use Quick Share (called Nearby Share on older phones): select the photos, tap Share, tap Quick Share, and pick your computer. Both methods use Wi-Fi Direct, so they are fast and they do not use your internet data. The limit is that they only work within the same family — AirDrop is Apple only, and Quick Share needs a Windows computer — so they are no good for an iPhone to a Windows PC.

4. Your phone's companion app: the middle ground

Both Apple and Google offer apps that link your phone to your computer. On Windows, the Phone Link app (called Link to Windows on Samsung phones) shows your recent photos on your computer, and you can drag them straight onto your desktop — no cable, no upload, and it works over Wi-Fi. Samsung phones also work with Samsung Flow, and many other brands include their own version. The set-up takes five minutes the first time: install the app on both devices, sign in with the same account, and pair them once. After that, your latest photos are always a click away on your computer, which makes this the best option if you move photos across every week or two.

5. Email or message them to yourself: only for a few photos

The simplest method of all is also the most limited: email the photos to yourself, or send them to yourself in WhatsApp or Telegram. It works on any phone and any computer, with nothing to install, and it is fine for one or two photos — a form that needs a picture, a photo a relative asked for. But do not use it for a whole album. Email apps shrink photos or refuse large attachments, and WhatsApp compresses photos unless you send them as a document, so the quality drops. Treat this as the quick fix for a couple of photos, not a way to back up your camera roll.

How to transfer photos from your phone to your computer: the short version

It depends on how many photos you have. For a big clear-out of hundreds of photos, use a cable — it is the fastest and it keeps the full quality. For a few photos a week, turn on Google Photos or iCloud backup and download them from the website when you need them. For a handful of photos right now, use AirDrop or Quick Share if your devices match. And whatever method you choose, do it before your phone runs out of space: a phone that is full stops taking photos at the worst possible moment, and a copy on your computer means your photos survive even if the phone is lost or broken.

The short version

  • A USB cable is fastest for hundreds of photos and keeps full quality; the phone appears as a drive on your computer.
  • Turn on Google Photos or iCloud backup once, and your photos upload automatically over Wi-Fi.
  • AirDrop (iPhone to Mac) and Quick Share (Android to Windows) move photos wirelessly in seconds.

Frequently asked questions

How do I transfer photos from my phone to my computer without a cable?

Use Google Photos or iCloud backup and download from the website, or use AirDrop (iPhone to Mac) or Quick Share (Android to Windows) for wireless transfer.

Why can't my computer see my phone when I plug it in?

On iPhone, tap "Trust This Computer" when asked. On Android, swipe down the notification and change the USB mode from "Charging only" to "File transfer".

Does transferring photos to my computer delete them from my phone?

No. Copying photos leaves the originals on your phone. Delete them from the phone only after you have checked the copies opened properly on your computer.

Is it better to use Google Photos or a cable?

Google Photos is easier because it is automatic, but the free storage is limited to 15GB. A cable is faster for hundreds of photos and costs nothing extra.
